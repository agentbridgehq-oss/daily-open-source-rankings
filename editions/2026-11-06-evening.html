Evening Edition — Friday, November 6, 2026

A Control Surface for Every Coding Agent, an IDE Rebuilt in Rust, and a File Converter That Does 263 Formats — Tonight's 10 Open-Source Breakouts

I'll be honest: tonight's board is a builders' board. The morning was all agent skills; the evening is the tooling underneath them — the control surfaces, the editors, the players, and the converters that make a developer's day actually work. Three of the ten are about taming coding agents, and the rest are the kind of practical, download-it-tonight utilities that spread by word of mouth.

1. pingdotgg/t3code (★49.3k, +1,457 today) — One control surface for every coding agent on your machine. If you've got Claude Code, Codex, and half a dozen other agents installed, t3code gives you a single place to drive them all. No more juggling terminals and wondering which agent is doing what. The T3 team built it for people who run agents the way other people run tabs, and nearly fifty thousand stars says that crowd is bigger than anyone guessed.

2. l0ng-ai/openidea (★7.4k, +1.3k today) — The IDE you know, rebuilt in Rust. IntelliJ-style keymap, tool windows, search-everywhere — editor, code insight, debugger, Git, terminal, database and HTTP clients for 60+ languages, all in a fast native app. Rebuilding an IDE from scratch is the kind of ambition most people talk about and never ship. This one is shipping, and it's gaining more than a thousand stars a day.

3. deepseek-ai/dsh-superagent-prototype (★20.2k, +793/day) — DeepSeek's push toward a more autonomous agent system. It's a prototype, not a product — the point is the architecture: agents that plan further, act on their own, and need less hand-holding. Twenty thousand stars on a prototype tells you how many people are watching this direction closely, because whoever cracks autonomy first changes the whole game.

4. livliv-dev/player (★954, +668/day) — A lightweight 2.5D character player for the web, with lip sync and webcam face tracking. Drop a character into a page and it talks, reacts, and follows your face. Two days old and already one of the fastest climbers on the board — streamers, educators, and anyone building a virtual presenter will see the appeal in about ten seconds.

5. Tianyu199509/DeskBox (★8.3k, +97 today) — A free, open-source Windows desktop organizer built on WinUI 3 widgets. Your desktop stops being a junk drawer and starts behaving like a workspace: files managed natively, right where you can see them. It's not flashy, and that's the point. The best desktop tools are the ones you forget are there.

6. umlx5h/LLPlayer (★4.6k, +69 today) — A media player built for language learning. Dual subtitles, AI-generated subtitles, real-time translation — watch anything and actually understand it. If you've ever paused a show every thirty seconds to look up a word, this is the player that was built for you.

7. milind-soni/OpenMausBot (★14k) — An open-source Grok Bot alternative with a virtual machine bots can use. The idea is simple: your own bot, your own model, your own sandbox — no waiting on someone else's platform to decide what your bot is allowed to do. Fourteen thousand stars and it keeps climbing, which says a lot about how many people want the bot without the leash.

8. KnifeLemon/Filee — A free, open-source file converter that handles 263 formats offline, by drag and drop. Images, PDF, Word, Excel, PowerPoint, video, audio, e-books, archives — no upload, no Office needed. We've all hit the moment where a file is in the wrong format and the "free" converter wants an account and a credit card. Filee just does the job, on your machine.

9. calesthio/OpenMontage — The world's first open-source, agentic video production system. Twelve production pipelines, 100+ tools, 700+ agent skill and production-knowledge files — it turns your AI coding assistant into a full video production studio. Video is the hardest medium to automate well, and a system this complete is exactly the kind of thing that quietly becomes infrastructure.

10. Sandflow-ai/sand3d (★783, +538/day) — 3D modeling in Python, built for LLMs. Instead of clicking through a modeling app, you describe the shape and code builds it. Three days old and already pulling in more than five hundred stars a day — because the moment models can make real 3D objects, every 3D workflow starts to look different.

That's your ten. My pick? t3code — because the agent era's biggest missing piece isn't a smarter model, it's a dashboard for the agents you already have, and the team that ships that first owns the workflow. If I've got an hour free, I'm playing with sand3d, because describing a 3D model into existence still feels like a trick, and tricks like that don't stay tricks for long.
