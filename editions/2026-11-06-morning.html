Morning Edition — Friday, November 6, 2026

A Skill That Un-Slops Your UI, Google's Own Agent Playbook, and Minecraft Rebuilt in Rust — Today's 10 Open-Source Breakouts

I'll be honest: this morning's board is a skills board. Four of the ten are agent skills — little instruction packs that make your coding agent noticeably better at one job — and the rest of the list is the kind of practical stuff that spreads fast: a scraper, a voice model, a video reverse-engineer, and a Minecraft rewrite that the Rust crowd has been waiting for.

1. heydamir/unslop (★9.6k, +1.2k today) — A Claude skill that takes a vibecoded, AI-looking interface and turns it into something that actually looks designed. You know the look: purple gradients, the same rounded cards, the same generic hero. Unslop teaches the agent taste — spacing, hierarchy, typography — and the result reads like a designer touched it. It only landed a week ago and it's already the fastest-rising new skill on the board.

2. D4Vinci/Scrapling (★97.7k, +1.7k today) — The web scraping framework that handles everything from a single request to a full-scale crawl. One library, one API, and it deals with the messy parts — retries, sessions, parsing — so you don't have to glue five tools together. Scraping is unglamorous work, but a tool this complete is why it keeps climbing.

3. garrytan/gstack (+612 today) — One Claude Code setup that works like an engineering team. Garry Tan's configuration turns a single agent into a crew: planning, building, reviewing, each with its own lane. If you've been running one agent at a time and wondering how people ship whole features with them, this is the shape of the answer.

4. scenario-labs/skills (+667 today) — Agent skills for making production-ready images, video, audio, and 3D. They pick the right model for the job, show you the price before anything gets spent, and keep characters and brands consistent across generations — plus expert teams that drive Blender, Maya, ZBrush, Unreal, and Unity. Media generation through agents usually falls apart on consistency. This is built to fix exactly that.

5. microsoft/VibeVoice (★56K, +223 today) — Microsoft's open-source voice AI family, with text-to-speech and speech recognition that can handle long-form audio in and out. Fifty-six thousand stars is not a flash in the pan — this is a serious model family, free to run, and the long-duration handling is the part most voice tools still fumble.

6. JuneYaooo/rea-video (★2K, +109 today) — Reverse-engineer any video. Feed it a clip — a livestream, an AI-generated video, a 3D animation — and it breaks it down shot by shot to show how it was made, then hands you a plan to recreate it. It's the video cousin of the reverse-engineering tools that have owned this board all week, and I can see why editors and creators will pass it around.

7. google/skills (+211 today) — Google's own agent skills, for Google products and Google Cloud. These are the instruction packs Google's teams use to get agents working with their own stack, now out in the open. When a company ships the way its own engineers work, it's worth reading even if you never touch their cloud.

8. vercel-labs/agent-browser (+150 today) — A browser CLI for AI agents, built on accessibility-tree snapshots. Your agent can actually see and drive a browser the way a person does — click, read, fill in forms — without screenshots and guesswork. Vercel keeps shipping the plumbing that makes computer-use agents real, and this is the browsing half of it.

9. JiaFeiMiao-K-Cat/DiscImageStudio (+984 today) — A tool for creating and burning images that are visible on the surface of a disc. Niche? Sure. But nearly a thousand stars in a day says there's a whole crowd of people who still burn discs and want them to look good doing it. Sometimes the breakout is the tool nobody else thought to build.

10. horizon-rust/horizon (★6.8K, +383 today) — Minecraft: Java Edition, rewritten from scratch in Rust, checked against vanilla with 281 differential test harnesses. That's not a weekend port — it's a full reimplementation with proof it behaves the same. The Rust community loves a rewrite done properly, and this one has the test suite to back up the ambition.

That's your ten. My pick? Unslop — because the difference between an interface that looks AI-made and one that looks designed is now a single skill install, and that's going to change what people expect from agent-built apps. If I've got an hour free, I'm running rea-video first, because watching a video get taken apart shot by shot is the fastest way I've seen to learn how good video actually gets made.
