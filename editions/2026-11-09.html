Daily Edition — Monday, November 9, 2026

Meta's Design System Goes Open, a Free Voice Studio, and the App Detective Still on Top — Today's 10 Open-Source Breakouts

I'll be honest: today's board has a split personality, and I like it. On one side, the giants are handing over the keys — Meta just open-sourced the design system it spent eight years building, and Cloudflare shipped a skill that turns your coding agent into a security auditor. On the other side, the little guys are doing the jobs you'd normally pay for: a voice studio that clones voices on your own machine, a dictation app that never sends a word to the cloud, and a Switch emulator that keeps your old saves. And sitting on top of all of it, still, is rea — the reverse-engineer that refuses to slow down.

1. morluto/rea (★445.5k, +1,931 today) — Point your agent at any app and learn how it works. It inspects native binaries through Hopper or Ghidra, JavaScript and Electron apps, .NET assemblies, and websites, then explains how a feature works — with the evidence, and the limits, behind each conclusion. The analysis runs locally. Four hundred and forty-five thousand stars, and it's still the fastest climber on the board. At this point it's not a trend, it's a landmark.

2. patchy631/ai-engineering-hub (★41.6k, +1,191 today) — The 93-project curriculum that turns AI curiosity into production skills. In-depth tutorials on LLMs, RAG, and real-world agent apps, organized from beginner to advanced, with OCR, voice agents, and MCP integrations in the mix. If you've been collecting agent tools without ever shipping anything, this is the homework that fixes that.

3. anomalyco/hex (★5.9k, +1,179 today) — Local voice dictation for Mac, Windows, and Linux, written in Rust. Hold a key, speak, and it pastes — the first run downloads a speech model and nothing ever leaves your machine. No account, no cloud, no subscription. The smallest star count on today's board and one of the fastest climbers, which tells you exactly what people want right now.

4. zortos293/Lichii (★7.2k, +1,072 today) — A very experimental Nintendo Switch and Switch 2 emulator, forked from the yuzu-line family. The rebrand keeps the old data directories and config keys, so your saves, settings, and shader caches carry over untouched. Builds exist for Windows, Linux, and macOS. Games, keys, and firmware are not included — bring your own, obviously.

5. cloudflare/security-audit-skill (★67.5k, +1,023 today) — Install it as a skill and your coding agent audits a codebase for vulnerabilities. It maps the architecture and trust boundaries first, sends isolated hunter agents after the uncovered areas, then hands every candidate to a fresh verifier that tries to disprove it — so a finding only reaches the report with a complete source trace. The audit that argues with itself before it argues with you.

6. ccch1mneyyy/dsh-Go (★17.1k, +990 today) — DeepSeek Harness, in your pocket. An unofficial Android client that installs an Ubuntu and Node.js environment inside the app, so the DSH engine runs on the phone itself — no root, no Termux — and it can also switch to a DSH instance over SSH. Chat covers model switching, images, voice input, and tool approvals. Your coding agent, riding along in your pocket.

7. Ebony-Vinyl/dsh-our-free-model (★67k, +975 today) — A plugin for the DeepSeek Harness shell: install it and the model picker fills with free frontier models like DeepSeek V4.1 Flash and Kimi K3 — no login, no API key. The free lanes come straight from public gateways, re-pulled every refresh, and a local relay port lets your other tools call the same models. Usage stats stay on the machine.

8. milind-soni/OpenMausBot (★19.2k, +926 today) — An open-source, local-first chat app in the spirit of Grok Bot, where every contact in the sidebar is a real agent: Claude or Codex running on your own machine, each with its own personality, model, cloud computer, and connected apps. You message them like friends, watch the work happen, and approve the steps that matter. It's the group chat where everyone in it actually does the work.

9. meta/astryx (★18.4k, +875 today) — Meta's open-source, agent-ready design system, built inside the company over eight years: 150+ accessible React components, brand theming, dark mode, ready-made templates, and a CLI as one cohesive system. Eight years of internal polish, now free for anyone to build on. If your side project has ever looked like a side project, this is the shortcut.

10. debpalash/VoiceStudio (★105.6k) — The open-source, fully-local ElevenLabs alternative: voice cloning, voice design, video dubbing, dictation, transcription, and audiobook creation in 646 languages. Everything runs on your hardware. A hundred thousand stars for a tool that replaces a paid subscription with a download — that number says more than I could.

That's your ten. My pick? hex — because a dictation app that never phones home is the kind of small, honest tool I end up using every single day, and it gained over a thousand stars in a day with barely six thousand total. That's the shape of something about to take off. If I've got an hour free, I'm running the ai-engineering-hub tutorials first, because the tools on this board are only worth what you can actually build with them.
