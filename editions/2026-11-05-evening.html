Evening Edition — Thursday, November 5, 2026

Cloudflare's Security Auditor Skill, a Free ElevenLabs Rival, and DeepSeek in Your Pocket — Tonight's 10 Open-Source Breakouts

I'll be honest: tonight's board is all about agents getting a second opinion. Cloudflare shipped a skill that makes your coding agent audit its own work, Google open-sourced the way its own engineers review a design before a line of code gets written, and DeepSeek's agent harness is now running on phones. The rest of the list is the stuff that makes the agent wave actually usable — free voices, a free tutor, and a desktop team that works while you sleep.

1. cloudflare/security-audit-skill — The strongest debut on the board tonight. Install it as a skill and your coding agent maps the codebase's architecture and trust boundaries, sends isolated hunter agents after the uncovered areas, then hands every candidate finding to a fresh verifier that tries to disprove it before anything reaches the report. That verifier step is the whole point: most AI security tools cry wolf, and this one argues with itself first.

2. pbakaus/impeccable (★103k, +9.4k/7d) — The design vocabulary for your coding agent. It teaches the agent taste: how to steer a design toward polish and review it with a sharper eye, so the interfaces it ships stop looking like a default template. Nine thousand stars in a week says a lot of people are tired of agent-built UIs that all look the same.

3. debpalash/VoiceStudio (★101k, +7.9k/7d) — The fully-local ElevenLabs alternative. Voice cloning, voice design, video dubbing, dictation, transcription, and audiobook creation in 646 languages, all on your own machine. No subscription, no audio leaving your computer, no monthly bill for a voice you already own.

4. deepseek-ai/deepseek-harness (★273k, +7.9k/7d) — "Everything is a Plugin." DeepSeek's harness for building a more autonomous agent system, and the plugin model is why it's moving this fast: the community keeps bolting new abilities onto it instead of waiting for the next official release.

5. ccch1mneyyy/dsh-Go (★11.3k, +1,996/day) — The harness, in your pocket. Run DeepSeek's agent setup from your phone and remote-control your computer with it. Your whole agent setup stops being chained to your desk, and at nearly two thousand stars a day, plenty of people clearly wanted exactly that.

6. Ebony-Vinyl/dsh-our-free-model (★62k, +12k/7d) — Install this plugin in dsh and you get a frontier-class model with no login, no sign-up, and no API key. That's the fastest-growing number on the board tonight, and I can see why: zero setup is a hell of a feature.

7. HKUDS/DeepTutor (★55k, +6.3k/7d) — Lifelong personalized tutoring, from the University of Hong Kong's data science lab. A tutor that remembers what you struggled with last month and builds on it, instead of starting from zero every single session. The kind of tool that gets better the longer you use it.

8. Orkas-AI/Orkas (★9.0k, +5.3k/7d) — A local-first AI team for your desktop, MIT licensed. A Commander breaks your goal into steps and hands them to 16 built-in specialists — research, writing, decks, video, office work — plus the coding agents you already have, all running as local sessions. Your files and keys stay on your machine, and the packaged app ships with 500 free credits so you can try the built-in model without handing over a key.

9. zortos293/Lichii (★1.8k, +1,144/day) — A very experimental Switch and Switch 2 emulator, forked from the yuzu line, and it keeps your old saves and settings through the switch. Small numbers, big pull — emulators always travel fast once word gets out, and over a thousand stars a day says the word is out.

10. google/elephant-goldfish-model (★12.3k) — Google's open-source plugin for spec-driven development. The Elephant — your main agent, full context — drafts the design. The Goldfish — fresh subagents with zero conversation history — try to break it across five review stages before any code exists. Thousands of Google engineers use it internally, and the rule is simple: no code until the plan survives review.

That's your ten. My pick? The Cloudflare skill — an agent that checks its own homework is the difference between a demo and something you'd trust with production code. If I've got an hour free, I'm installing VoiceStudio first, because a free local voice clone in 646 languages is the kind of tool that pays for itself by Friday.
