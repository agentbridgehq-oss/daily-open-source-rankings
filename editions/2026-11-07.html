Daily Edition — Saturday, November 7, 2026

A Free Studio for 600 AI Models, a Fleet of Coding Agents, and a Dictation App That Never Phones Home — Today's 10 Open-Source Breakouts

I'll be honest: today's board is a do-it-yourself board. The biggest names on it aren't asking you to sign up for anything — they're handing you the whole machine and letting you run it on your own hardware. A free studio covering 600 image and video models, a fleet of coding agents you drive yourself, a security center that needs zero API keys, and a dictation app that never sends a word to the cloud. The rest is the gear that makes the DIY stack actually work — fine-tuning from a single file, resumes that write themselves, and a WhatsApp client built for speed.

1. Anil-matcha/Open-Generative-AI (★32.8K, +398 today) — A free, open-source AI image and video generation studio that supports over 600 models, self-hosted under an MIT license. Six hundred models under one roof, no subscription, no per-image bill. If you've been paying a monthly fee to generate images, this is the repo that makes you ask what the fee was for.

2. stablyai/orca (★117k, +6.6k/7d) — Orca runs a fleet of coding agents — Claude Code, Codex, and the rest — with your own models and your own rules. One operator, many agents, all working in parallel. The agent era keeps producing fleets and dashboards because nobody wants to babysit one agent at a time anymore.

3. patchy631/ai-engineering-hub (★40.5k, +1,191/day) — Ninety-three production-ready AI projects in one repo: OCR, RAG, agentic workflows, MCP integrations, voice agents — organized by difficulty, beginner to advanced. It's a curriculum disguised as a repo, and it's gaining more than a thousand stars a day because people want to learn this stuff by building it, not by reading about it.

4. beenuar/AiSOC (★6.4k, +446/day) — An open-source AI Security Operations Center running 2,538 executable detection rules from a 7,023-rule library, with a local LLM doing the agent triage and every prompt and tool call recorded in a replayable Investigation Ledger. Zero API keys required. A SOC you can run at home is a sentence that would have sounded strange two years ago; today it's just Saturday's trending list.

5. anomalyco/hex (★3.5k, +1,179/day) — A Rust-based local voice dictation app for macOS, Windows, and Linux. Hold a key, speak, and it pastes — the first run downloads a speech model and nothing ever leaves your machine. More than a thousand stars a day for a dictation app says a lot of people are done sending their voice to someone else's server.

6. AienTech/dossier (★4.8k, +1.3k/7d) — A self-hosted job search and resume builder that writes tailored resumes and cover letters for each application. Your job hunt, your data, your machine — and the resume actually matches the posting instead of the generic one you've been sending everywhere.

7. MakazhanAlpamys/Soup (★10k, +433/7d) — Fine-tune LLMs from one YAML file. Layer streaming trains an 8B model on a 4 GB laptop GPU. Fine-tuning used to mean a cloud bill and a weekend of setup; now it's a config file and a laptop that fits in a backpack.

8. HKUDS/nanobot (★50k, +203/7d) — Your lightweight open-source personal agent, from the University of Hong Kong's data science lab. Small, fast, and personal — the opposite of the giant agent platforms that want to own your whole workflow.

9. crmne/zapfast (★3.1K, +296 today) — ZapFast is a fast, native WhatsApp client in Rust and egui. No Electron, no browser tab eating your RAM — just a quick native app that opens fast and stays out of the way. Sometimes the breakout isn't a new idea at all; it's the old idea done properly.

10. tembo/voxl (★1.2K, +214 today) — Extremely fast web remote desktop streaming. Your desktop, in a browser tab, with the lag squeezed out. Remote work keeps getting more fluid, and the tools that remove the friction between you and your machine keep climbing.

That's your ten. My pick? Open-Generative-AI — because 600 models, self-hosted, free, is the kind of math that ends subscriptions. If I've got an hour free, I'm installing Hex, because talking to my computer and having the words just appear still feels like the future, and this one does it without phoning home.
