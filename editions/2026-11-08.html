Daily Edition — Sunday, November 8, 2026

Reverse-Engineer Any App, an Agent That Never Forgets, and Zelda Rebuilt for PC — Today's 10 Open-Source Breakouts

I'll be honest: today's board is a detective board. The two fastest climbers both point your coding agent at software you didn't write and ask it to figure out how it works — one reads the binaries, the other rebuilds the whole thing clean-room style. Around them sits the stuff that makes the agent wave actually stick: memory that survives between sessions, a skill pack from the person who teaches TypeScript to the internet, and a way to check whether AI assistants even mention your product. And then there's Zelda, running natively on a PC with no emulator in sight.

1. morluto/rea (★441k, +1,931 today) — Point your agent at any app and learn how it works. It inspects native binaries through Hopper or Ghidra, JavaScript and Electron apps, .NET assemblies, and websites, then explains how a feature works — with the evidence, and the limits, behind each conclusion. The analysis runs locally. Four hundred and forty-one thousand stars is not a debut, and it's still the fastest climber on the board.

2. storytold/craft-anything (★21.1k, +2,507/day) — Study how any app or game works, then have an agent rebuild it. A clean-room engine in pure Rust: it studies the original with real inspection tools, then your coding agent builds your version in the stack you name and refines it against the original. Early alpha, 314 tools, and the second-fastest growth on GitHub right now.

3. thedotmack/claude-mem (★114.6k, +7.5k this week) — Persistent context for every agent. It captures everything your agent does in a session, compresses it with AI, and injects the relevant bits back into future sessions. Works with Claude Code, OpenClaw, Codex, Gemini, and more. The agent that forgets everything overnight is the one you have to re-explain your whole project to every morning. This one remembers.

4. NVIDIA/auto-ontology (★7.2k, +2,707 today) — NVIDIA's way to build semantic reasoning layers over your data, so LLM and agent workflows can actually explain themselves. The single biggest daily gain on today's board, and it comes from NVIDIA, which means the enterprise crowd is already reading it.

5. mattpocock/skills (★318k, +2,053 today) — A small set of reusable instructions that make coding agents work like a careful senior engineer instead of guessing. Matt Pocock teaches TypeScript to hundreds of thousands of developers, and this is the skill pack distilled from how he actually works. Three hundred eighteen thousand stars says the "just tell the agent to be careful" era is over.

6. sherlock-project/sherlock (+1,361 today) — Check one username across more than 400 social networks. Hand it a name and it hunts down every account registered under it, writing each match to a file, CSV, or spreadsheet. Run several names at once, restrict it to specific sites, or route it through a proxy. It's the OSINT staple — and it just climbed two spots overnight.

7. Albert-Weasker/niubigeo (+1,208 today) — Check whether AI assistants recommend your product. A self-hosted tool that tests how different models describe your product: enter a domain, pick models, and it runs keyword tests showing who appears in answers and which sources get cited. SEO used to be about Google's first page. Now it's about what the chatbot says when someone asks for a recommendation.

8. NotRexed/Hookshot (★2.1k, +1,640/day) — A native recompilation of the Ocarina of Time Switch 2 remake. The game code is translated to C and compiled into a native Windows or Linux program, graphics rebuilt on Vulkan. You build it from your own copy in about 40 minutes, no emulator involved. Sound, saves, and the in-game menu already work. Two days old, sixteen hundred stars a day, and the most fun project on the board by a mile.

9. Nutlope/hallmark (+6,994 this week) — AI websites that don't look AI-made. Hallmark steers your agent away from the purple-gradient, same-rounded-cards look and toward something that reads as actually designed. Nearly seven thousand stars this week says a lot of people are tired of interfaces that all look the same.

10. wheresryan22/anatomy (trending #2 this week) — An agent skill for detailed, interactive isometric illustrations of real-world machines and complex systems. It generates polished SVGs, with optional WebGL, that break a machine apart and show how the pieces fit. The most visual entry on the board.

That's your ten. My pick? rea — because the moment your agent can read any app's binaries and explain how it works, the whole idea of "closed source" starts to feel like a suggestion. If I've got an hour free, I'm running claude-mem first, because an agent that remembers what we built yesterday is the difference between a tool and a teammate.
